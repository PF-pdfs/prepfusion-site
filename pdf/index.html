Some years show noticeably more questions than others — often because GATE ran that branch's exam across more than one real sitting that year, so more distinct question papers get counted. Other tall bars can just mean more questions were asked that year, with no extra sitting involved. Years before 2010 also asked a different total number of questions per paper (85 in 2005-2008, 60 in 2009, 65 from 2010 onward) — so a taller bar from that era can reflect the format of the year, not extra emphasis on the topic.

Where shown: WT (Avg Weightage) is the average marks this chapter contributed per real GATE sitting — not a raw sum across every year, which would overcount a chapter that happened to appear in a multi-session year. Range is the lowest and highest marks it contributed in any single sitting, with the year each occurred. Both are computed from 2010 onward only for the same reason as the chart above, and depend on knowing which years ran more than one real sitting — that research is thorough for some branches and still ongoing for others, so treat both figures as a best-effort estimate.
